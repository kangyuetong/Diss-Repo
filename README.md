# Modelling Indigenous Heuristics and Influence on Sourcing Policies in Southeast Asia
## Diss repo for future reference
### Abstract 
We are often said to be living in the Anthropocene, a geological epoch defined by the scale and pace of human-induced environmental change now inscribed in the Earth's strata. Corporate supply chains are among its most consequential drivers. The extraction of natural resources to sustain global commodity demand has driven deforestation, displaced Indigenous communities, and generated ecological deterioration at a rate that now threatens the commercial systems dependent upon it. Corporate sustainability has emerged as a strategic response to these pressures. Yet many sustainability initiatives remain largely symbolic, prioritising procedural compliance without producing the structural ecological change they purport to achieve. This study investigates the structural conditions that perpetuate this disconnect and identifies pathways for transformative sourcing through a systems-thinking lens, using Causal Loop Diagrams. The study thereby advances a dynamic, systems-informed understanding of how structural patterns shape corporate sustainability outcomes, and provides practical and actionable pathways for corporations to transition from symbolic compliance toward long-term ecological stewardship.
